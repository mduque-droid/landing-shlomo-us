Executive Summary

RECS (Regulatory Education & Certification System) is the integrated platform through which your organization administers, delivers, monitors, and certifies training programs that lead to State and Federal licenses. Within RECS, each License defines the legal rules of a program — validity period, daily hour limits, program manager, and required identity verification modes — and groups one or more Specialties (with minimum hours, validity years, and CEU credits). Each Specialty is composed of Modules and Lessons and is delivered through Courses. RECS monitors students in real time via Active Proctoring — profile photo, facial recognition, valid ID, email, phone, and address — and only issues a Certificate once effective hours, verified attendance, and identity requirements are fully met, leaving an immutable audit trail for regulatory authorities.

1. Platform Overview & Purpose

RECS (Regulatory Education & Certification System) is your organization's unified workspace to administer, deliver, monitor, and certify training programs leading to State and Federal licenses.

From a single Control Panel, your team can:

  • Configure the legal rules of each license (validity period, minimum hours, daily limits, and required identity verifications).
  • Design the course catalog: Specialties, Courses, Modules, and Lessons.
  • Monitor each student in real time through Active Proctoring, ensuring the person studying is actually the enrolled individual — without requiring constant manual supervision.
  • Audit, issue, and renew certificates that are valid before regulatory bodies, backed by a dispute-proof evidence record.
📌 Note RECS is built on a compliance-by-design principle: the system will not issue a certificate unless all legally configured requirements have been verifiably met. This protects your organization against audits and disputes.

2. Operational Glossary

Before operating RECS, familiarize yourself with these five core concepts and how they relate to each other.

2.1 What is a License (State or Federal)?

A License is the "rules contract" that defines how a training program must legally be fulfilled. It is the highest-level entity in RECS. Each License stores:

Field on screenOperational meaning
NameOfficial name of the license.
Type (State / Federal)Indicates which regulatory authority governs the program.
Legal descriptionRegulatory text printed on the certificate.
Training validity (months)Maximum time the student has to complete the training before it expires.
Daily hour limitMaximum hours a student can accumulate per day (a common legal requirement).
Program Manager (name & signature)The responsible person who endorses and signs the certificates.
Required Verification ModesWhich identity checks will be applied (see section 2.4).
📌 Note The State / Federal distinction determines which issuing body recognizes the program and typically implies stricter verification rules at the Federal level.

2.2 How is a Specialty defined?

A Specialty is the specific credential the student will earn. It always belongs to a License (inheriting its legal rules) and adds its own requirements:

Field on screenOperational meaning
Name & CodeSpecialty identifier (the code is usually required by the regulator).
Training hoursMinimum effective hours the student must accumulate to pass.
Validity yearsHow long the credential remains valid once issued (basis for renewal).
CEU / CreditsContinuing education credits awarded.
In-person modalityIndicates whether the specialty requires in-person attendance.
PriceEnrollment cost.

2.3 How is a Course linked to a Specialty to grant or renew a License?

The value chain follows this hierarchy. Understanding it is the key to operating RECS:

LICENSE (legal rules: validity, hours, verifications)
   └─ SPECIALTY (credential: minimum hours, validity, CEU)
          └─ MODULES (thematic units, ordered)
                 └─ LESSONS (video content + text)
                        └─ ASSESSMENTS (questions & answers)

COURSE ──(linked to one or more)──► SPECIALTIES
  • The Specialty contains the mandatory content (Modules and Lessons) and the minimum hours required by regulation.
  • The Course is the "commercial and academic vehicle" through which a student enrolls; it is linked to the specialties it enables.
  • When the student completes a specialty — meeting hours + assessments + identity verifications — RECS issues the certificate accrediting the license. Renewal is managed based on the specialty's Validity years.
📌 Note A single Specialty can be linked to multiple Courses, and a Course can enable multiple Specialties. This lets you reuse content without duplicating work.

2.4 What is Active Proctoring?

Active Proctoring is the set of identity checks that RECS requires and verifies automatically. They are configured at the License level and may include:

Verification ModeWhat it involves
Profile PhotoThe student submits a headshot photo, automatically validated (face visible, frontal, no glasses or hat, clear background).
Facial RecognitionDuring study sessions, the student's live face is periodically compared against the registered profile photo.
Identity Document (Valid ID)The student uploads an official document (front and back), which is reviewed and approved.
EmailVerified via a confirmation code.
PhoneVerified via a confirmation code.
AddressConfirmation of the registered home address.

3. Step-by-Step Operational Workflows

3.1 Licenses & Specialties Module

Access Log in to the RECS Control Panel and open the Licenses Section (requires Configuration permission). Specialties are managed in the Specialties Module.

A. Creating a License and configuring its rules

  1. In the Licenses Section, click "Create new License".
  2. Enter the Name and select the Type: State or Federal.
  3. Write the Legal description that will appear on the certificate and the Program Manager name.
  4. Set the compliance rules:
    • Training validity (months) — deadline to complete.
    • Daily hour limit — maximum hours allowed per day.
  5. In the Verification tab, enable the required Verification Modes (Profile Photo, Facial Recognition, Valid ID, Email, Phone, Address).
  6. Upload the logo and manager's signature that will appear on certificates.
  7. Associate the license with the Sub-school(s) (branch or institution) that will offer it.
  8. Save. The license becomes Active and available for creating specialties.
⚠ Warning Verification Modes must be configured before enrolling students. If Facial Recognition or Profile Photo is enabled, the student cannot start training until their profile photo is approved.

B. Creating a Specialty and linking courses

  1. Open the Specialties Module and click "New Specialty".
  2. Select the License it belongs to (inherits its legal rules).
  3. Fill in Name, Code, Training hours (minimum), Validity years, CEU, and Price.
  4. Indicate whether it is In-person.
  5. Build the mandatory content: add Modules (in order) and, within each, the Lessons (with video and text) and their Assessments.
  6. Go to the Courses Module, create or edit a Course, and in the Specialties tab, link it to this specialty.
  7. Activate the specialty (Status: Active) to publish it.
📌 Note The Training hours defined here are the legal minimum. RECS will not certify a student who has not accumulated that amount of effective study time.

3.2 Live Student Monitoring (Active Proctoring)

Active Proctoring works automatically while the student studies. Your team does not need to watch manually: RECS monitors, records, and acts on its own. Here's how:

At the start of a study session

  1. RECS checks all verification modes required by the license (email, phone, address, profile photo, ID, face).
  2. If any requirement is unmet (e.g., the profile photo is not approved), study cannot begin and the student is told what to correct.

During the study session

  1. If the license requires Facial Recognition, RECS requests a live photo periodically (approximately every 5 minutes).
  2. That photo is automatically compared against the registered profile photo and must reach a high confidence match to be considered valid.
  3. The daily hour limit is also enforced: when the student reaches the limit set by the license, RECS stops counting time for that day.

Audit log (automatic)

Every check — successful or failed — is saved in the student's Verification History, including the mode checked, the result, the reason for failure (if any), and the date and time. This history is the evidence that backs the certificate's legal validity.

📌 Note RECS distinguishes between an identity failure (face does not match) and a technical issue (camera not permitted, blurry image). Only identity failures affect compliance; technical issues are flagged separately so students are not unfairly penalized.

3.3 Auditing, Issuing, and Renewing Licenses

Access Reports Module and the student's record in the Student Management Module.

A. Checking the status of a training

In the student's record you will see the Training Status, which can be:

StatusOperational meaning
Not startedEnrolled but has not yet begun studying.
In progressActive; accumulating hours and verifications.
CompletedAll requirements met; certificate issued.
Not passedDid not meet requirements (time, assessment, or validity expired).

RECS also shows the completion percentage, effective hours accumulated, and the training expiration date.

B. Verifying compliance and issuing the certificate

Before issuing, RECS automatically runs a legal compliance checklist. The certificate is only issued if all conditions are met:

  • ✓ The training is not already completed or expired.
  • ✓ The payment / enrollment is complete.
  • ✓ Effective hours meet the specialty's minimum.
  • ✓ Identity is verified according to the required modes (approved profile photo, valid ID uploaded, contact verified).

Upon issuance, RECS generates a PDF certificate with a QR code and saves a "Validation Snapshot": a frozen copy of all evidence (verified identity, hours met, minimum time, document, specialty, and license) at the exact moment of issuance.

📌 Note The Validation Snapshot is your best asset in an audit: it reflects the rules and evidence as they were at the time of certification, even if the configuration changes later. The QR code allows any regulatory authority to verify the certificate's authenticity.

C. Downloading certificates and reports

In the Reports Module you can download:

  • Individual student Certificates (PDF with QR).
  • Reports for Students, Courses, Operators, Invoices, and Sub-school.

D. License renewal

The credential's validity period is governed by the specialty's Validity years. To renew, re-enroll the student in the corresponding specialty; RECS will again require hours and verifications to be met before issuing the renewed certificate.

5. Incident Handling & FAQ

❓ What to do if a student is blocked by Active Proctoring?

A block occurs when an identity verification is not passed. Follow these steps:

  1. Open the student's Verification History (student record → verification section).
  2. Identify the reason logged:
Reason shownRecommended action
Face does not matchAsk the student to retake the photo in good lighting, facing forward. If it persists, verify that the registered profile photo is correct.
Profile photo not approvedInstruct the student to re-upload it following the rules (frontal face, no glasses/hat, clear background, sharp image).
Technical issue (camera, blurry image)Not a compliance failure. Ask the student to check camera permissions and try again.
Identity document pending / rejectedReview the uploaded document and approve it or request a legible one.
Email or phone not verifiedResend the validation code from the student's record.

Once the requirement is corrected, the student can resume the session normally.

⚠ Warning Do not manually issue or force certificates to "unblock" a student. RECS blocks by design to protect the legal validity of the certificate. Always resolve the root cause of the failed verification.

❓ How to manage alerts for licenses nearing expiration or inactive?

  • Training nearing expiration: check the Reports / Students Module for trainings still In progress whose expiration date is approaching. Contact the student to complete their hours before the deadline.
  • Credential up for renewal: use the specialty's Validity years to anticipate renewals and re-enroll the student on time.
  • Inactive licenses or specialties: in the Licenses Section / Specialties Module, a Status: Inactive prevents new enrollments. Reactivate only when the legal configuration is current and verified.
📌 Note Always keep the Program Manager name and signature up to date in each license — they are printed on certificates and must correspond to the currently responsible person.
